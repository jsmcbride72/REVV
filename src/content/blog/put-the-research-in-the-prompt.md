Two announcements in the same week made a quiet shift in research tools hard to ignore.

On November 5, 2026, Auros, the company behind UserTesting and User Interviews, made its UserTesting MCP server available through the SpaceXSI Marketplace for teams using Grok Build. The server supports participant recruitment, study creation and launch, customer feedback retrieval, and checking AI-generated concepts with real people. Auros describes a network of more than 7.6 million verified people, including 3.2 million professionals across 140 industries. Eric Johnson, the company's CEO, said the next generation of AI-powered products will be shaped by teams that combine the speed of AI with continuous learning, without treating research and validation as separate steps.

Five days later, on November 10, Userlytics released a connection from its research platform to Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini. Teams can explore study goals, existing AI Insights and reports, participant transcripts, and quantitative results in the assistant they already have open. They do not have to export a deck and upload it somewhere else. The uses Userlytics lists are the ones a product team actually reaches for: compare themes, ratings, NPS, and time on task across studies; ask a follow-up about a finding that already exists; and pull participant quotes with timestamps and links back to the recording.

The permission details are as important as the feature list. Access stays inside the studies available to the connected account. Participants appear by first name and last initial. Last names, emails, tester profiles, and billing data are not shared. Jorge Baez, Userlytics' CTO, described the aim as analysis that can travel into other tools and still stay anchored to a single source of truth. Being open and being trusted, he said, should not be a trade-off.

That is the working rule we would tape above the prompt box. If the model cannot point at the clip, it does not get to settle the argument. A quote without a path back to the session is just a fluent sentence. Links to the recording are what keep a fast answer honest.

Both releases land in the same week because the bottleneck moved. Coding agents shortened the build. The scarce thing is no longer a first draft of the interface. It is knowing whether that draft is about the right problem. Pasting a PDF into a chat window was the workaround. A connection that can recruit, launch a study, and return evidence is a different shape of work. Research sits next to the prompt instead of after the sprint review.

None of this replaces a researcher, and none of it turns a weak question into a strong one. Userlytics still expects teams to design, launch, and analyze studies on its own platform. The MCP connection extends that work. It does not invent the study. Auros still depends on a person deciding what to test before anyone is recruited. The protocol is a pipe. What moves through it is whatever the team was disciplined enough to collect.

There is a practical order to this. Ask for the earlier finding before you ask for the screen. If shipping-cost doubts showed up in a previous checkout study, the agent helping you sketch the next one should be able to say so, and should be able to show the moment in the recording. Then watch the clip. The summary is a starting point. The participant is the source.

If you are pairing a coding agent with a design system this month, pair it with the study library too. The speed is already here. The judgment still has to be yours.
